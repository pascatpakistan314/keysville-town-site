## Part 1. What actually governs a Virginia town's handling of resident data

### Applies

| Rule | What it requires of Keysville | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Government Data Collection and Dissemination Practices Act**, Va. Code § 2.2-3800 et seq. | "Agency" expressly includes "any unit of local government including counties, cities, towns". § 2.2-3803(A): collect only what the law permits or a proper town purpose needs; collect from the person directly where possible; keep it accurate; keep a list of who has regular access; keep access records 3 years; train staff; "maintain safeguards against reasonably foreseeable threats"; never collect political or religious information without a statute or ordinance. § 2.2-3803(B): every public body website must post, "in a conspicuous manner", a privacy statement saying (i) what information is collected, (ii) what is collected automatically, (iii) whether cookies are set and why, (iv) how the information is used. | [§ 2.2-3801](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter38/section2.2-3801/), [§ 2.2-3803](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter38/section2.2-3803/) |
| **SSN restrictions**, § 2.2-3808 | An agency "shall not collect from an individual the social security number or any portion thereof" unless state or federal law authorizes or requires it **and** it is essential to the agency's duties. No service may be denied for refusing to give one. Nothing may be mailed with an SSN visible. Knowing violation is a misdemeanor; anyone may sue for an injunction and fees. The federal Privacy Act § 7 adds: any state or local agency that asks for an SSN must say, at the time it asks, whether it is mandatory or voluntary, under what authority, and how it will be used. **Consequence for the water application:** even the optional last-four field in the demo is a "portion" of an SSN. Unless the town can name the statute that authorizes it and show why the account cannot be opened without it, the field comes out. Our recommendation is to remove it from the online form and let the office verify identity in person with photo ID when the deposit is paid. | [§ 2.2-3808](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter38/section2.2-3808/), [DOJ, Privacy Act § 7](https://www.justice.gov/opcl/overview-privacy-act-1974-2020-edition/ssn) |
| **Virginia FOIA**, § 2.2-3700 et seq. | Everything a resident sends through a form is a public record the day it arrives. Release is the rule; exemptions are discretionary and read narrowly. | **Breach notification**, § 18.2-186.6 | "Entity" expressly includes governments and governmental subdivisions. Triggered when unencrypted, unredacted *name + SSN, driver's license, financial account number with its code, passport or military ID* is taken and the town reasonably believes it will cause identity theft or fraud. Notice to each affected Virginia resident **and the Attorney General** "without unreasonable delay"; also to the consumer reporting agencies if more than 1,000 people. Notice must say what happened in general terms, what data, what the town did, a phone number, and advice to watch statements and credit reports. Encrypted or redacted data (last four digits only) is outside the definition. AG penalty up to $150,000 per breach. **Because the site collects none of those identifiers, a breach of form data would not trigger this statute; it would still trigger the next row.** | [§ 18.2-186.6](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title18.2/chapter6/section18.2-186.6/) |

Report at reportcyber.virginia.gov. The Fusion Center passes it to VITA. The same section bars public bodies from using products DHS has banned for federal systems, and § 2.2-5514.1 bans TikTok and WeChat on town devices. **There is no § 2.2-5515**; Chapter 55.3 has only §§ 2.2-5514 and 2.2-5514.1. | [§ 2.2-5514](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacode/title2.2/chapter55.3/section2.2-5514/), [Chapter 55.3](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodefull/title2.2/chapter55.3), [Williams Mullen note](https://www.williamsmullen.com/insights/news/legal-news/new-cyber-incident-reporting-requirements-go-effect-virginia), [reportcyber.virginia.gov](https://www.reportcyber.virginia.gov/faqs) |

### Does not apply (and why it matters to say so)

| Rule | Status for Keysville |
|---|---|
| **HIPAA** | Binds covered entities (health plans, health care clearinghouses, and health care providers that transmit health information electronically for a standard transaction) and their business associates. A town is covered only if it runs one of those, for example a self-funded employee health plan or an EMS service that bills insurers electronically. Keysville has no health department, no EMS billing and, as far as we know, no self-funded plan [confirm]. So HIPAA does **not** bind the town website. The accommodation form and the broadband survey do touch health-adjacent facts (a disability, "prevented from accessing healthcare"); treat them as sensitive under § 2.2-3800 and FOIA judgment, not under HIPAA. Do not write "HIPAA-compliant" anywhere on the site. ([UNC School of Government](https://canons.sog.unc.edu/should-a-local-government-be-a-hipaa-hybrid-entity/)) |
| **CJIS Security Policy** | Applies to agencies that access criminal justice information (NCIC, criminal history). Keysville has no police department and the employment form asks about convictions in the applicant's own words, not from a records check. Not applicable. If the town ever runs fingerprint-based background checks through the State Police, that process (not the website) falls under CJIS. ([FBI CJIS Security Policy](https://le.fbi.gov/file-repository/cjis_security_policy_v5-9-1_20221001.pdf)) |
| **COPPA** | Commercial operators only; the FTC has said it does not reach state and local governments. The site is not directed to children anyway. (compliance.md) |
| **Section 508** | Federal agencies. Use "WCAG 2.1 AA under ADA Title II" instead. |
| **Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act** (§ 59.1-575 et seq.) | Expressly excludes "any body, authority, board, bureau, commission, district, or agency of the Commonwealth or of any political subdivision". Not applicable. |
| **GDPR** | No establishment in the EU and no services aimed at EU residents. Not applicable. |

## Part 2(a). Data inventory: every form in the demo

Sensitivity classes: **public record** (release on request), **personal** (name and contact details; a public record but handle with care, redact third-party details), **sensitive** (could harm the person if exposed: tax figures, conviction, disability, salary history, a minor), **never collect** (full SSN, DOB, driver's license, bank or card numbers, full account numbers, race, religion, politics).

The warning above the submit button on every form that takes personal data is the shared core string `form_foia` followed by the link `form_privacy`:

> **English:** What you send to the town may be a public record under Virginia law. Please don’t include Social Security or bank numbers. [How we handle your information]
> **Spanish:** Lo que envíe al pueblo puede ser un registro público según la ley de Virginia. No incluya números de Seguro Social ni de cuentas bancarias. [Cómo manejamos su información]

On the six online forms it appears on the review step, directly above Submit, after the signature block. Forms that take no personal data (site search, request lookup, trash-day lookup) carry no warning.

## Part 2(b). Rules for the real build

1. **Encryption in transit.** TLS 1.2 or better only, HSTS with `max-age=63072000; includeSubDomains; preload`, domain submitted to the HSTS preload list, HTTP redirects to HTTPS, no mixed content. Certificates renew automatically and expiry is monitored.
2. **Encryption at rest.** Full-disk encryption on the host; database-level encryption for the submissions table; uploaded files stored outside the web root on encrypted storage; backups encrypted with a key the hosting vendor does not hold.
3. **No form data in email.** Staff receive a notification that says "New zoning permit application K-1052 from [name]" with a link into Town Desk. The body never carries the answers, the file or the phone number. Email is unencrypted at rest in most mailboxes and is the first thing a phished account leaks.
4. **Role-based access in Town Desk.** Five roles (matrix in Part 2(d)). The role decides which form queues a person sees. Employment applications are visible to Admin and the hiring role only. Gross receipts are visible to Treasurer and Admin only. Council is read-only everywhere and sees no personal fields.
5. **MFA on every account**, staff and vendor, with an authenticator app or hardware key preferred and SMS as fallback. Admin accounts require it; the demo's two-step sign-in is the pattern. No shared logins; the generic `townoffice@` mailbox is never a Town Desk login.
6. **Sessions.** Idle timeout 30 minutes for staff, 15 for Admin, absolute 12 hours; cookies `Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax`; sign-out on password change; one warning two minutes before timeout with a keyboard-reachable "stay signed in".
7. **Audit log.** Every sign-in, failed sign-in, role change, view or export of a submission, redaction, release, deletion and settings change is logged with who, what, when and from where, kept 3 years (§ 2.2-3803(A)(7)), append-only, exportable as CSV from Town Desk, and itself exempt from FOIA under § 2.2-3705.2 where it would reveal access-control features.
8. **Least privilege.** Town Desk users cannot reach WordPress themes, plugins or settings. The developer has a separate admin account used only for deploys. Vendor support access is granted per ticket and expires. Quarterly access review: Admin confirms each account is still a current employee; the Security screen shows reviews that are due.
9. **Backups and restore tests.** Nightly encrypted off-site backups (database, uploads, configuration), 35 daily and 12 monthly copies, a quarterly restore test onto a staging copy, with the date and result shown on the Security screen and written to the activity log. A backup nobody has restored is a hope, not a backup.
10. **WAF and rate limiting.** A managed web application firewall in front of WordPress; `wp-login` and `xmlrpc.php` blocked at the edge for the public; REST user enumeration and author archives off; login attempts limited to 5 per 15 minutes per account and per IP; form posts limited to 10 per hour per IP; `wp-admin` reachable only from the staff network or through the WAF's access rule.
11. **Bots without CAPTCHA.** CAPTCHAs fail WCAG 2.2 for many residents. Use: a honeypot field hidden from people but visible to bots; a server-side timing check (reject posts under 3 seconds after the form rendered); a signed one-time form token; rate limits above; and a review queue, so a wrong guess costs a staff click, not a resident their request.
12. **Uploads.** Accept only PDF, JPEG, PNG and HEIC (converted to JPEG) up to 15 MB; check the type by content, not by extension; scan with ClamAV or the host's scanner before staff can open it; strip EXIF (GPS, device) from images; rename to a random id; store outside the web root with `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff` and no execute permission; serve through a download handler that checks the role.
13. **Redaction before FOIA release.** Town Desk has a "Prepare for release" view: it shows the submission with personal contact fields, third-party names (references, supervisors, guardians), tax figures and the minor flag pre-marked; the Clerk confirms each redaction, the system records the exemption cited (§ 2.2-3705.1(1), § 2.2-3705.7(1) or (7)), and the released copy is a flattened PDF, never the live record. The log keeps who released what to whom.
14. **Data minimization.** No full SSN anywhere. No last-four unless the town attorney names the statute (then Privacy Act § 7 notice, encrypted field, Treasurer-only). No date of birth unless a program requires it (none of the six forms do). Account numbers masked to the last four in every list view. Employer details on the water form made optional and dropped if the town does not use them. Demographic questions stay off the broadband survey. Retention labels on every record so purging follows the schedule.
15. **Incident response.** A one-page plan kept on paper in Town Hall and in Town Desk: (1) contain (disable the account, take the site to maintenance mode, keep the logs); (2) within 24 hours of discovery, report to the Virginia Fusion Intelligence Center at reportcyber.virginia.gov (§ 2.2-5514), with the hosting vendor and Pascat on the call; (3) decide with the town attorney whether § 18.2-186.6 personal information was taken; if so, notify affected residents and the Attorney General without unreasonable delay, and the credit bureaus if more than 1,000; (4) notify the cyber insurer [confirm policy]; (5) restore from the last clean backup, rotate every credential and key; (6) write the after-action note and log it. Contacts: Town Manager, Town Clerk, hosting vendor, Pascat, town attorney, VSP Fusion Center 804-674-2196 [confirm], CISA report line.
16. **Vendors.** Each vendor gets a written data-handling expectation and a copy of the retention rule. **Paymentus:** hosts payment; the town redirects to it and never embeds it; keep the AOC on file; confirm what customer data flows back (account number, amount, confirmation), and that none of it lands in email. **Hosting (WordPress host or managed provider):** US data centers, encrypted at rest, nightly backups, WAF, patching within 30 days (critical within 7), incident notice to the town within 24 hours, no use of town data for anything else. **SMS and email provider for alerts:** stores only phone, email, language and topics; opt-out honored at once; the subscriber list is exportable and deletable by the town; TCPA consent recorded. **Pascat:** maintenance access through a named account with MFA, no production data copied to laptops, test data only in staging.
17. **Email authentication.** SPF with `-all`, DKIM on every sending service, DMARC at `p=reject` with aggregate reports to a mailbox someone reads; a DMARC `p=reject` record on any town domain that does not send mail (townofkeysvilleva.com if still owned); MTA-STS where the mail host supports it.
18. **HSTS preload and headers.** Besides HSTS: `Content-Security-Policy` with no inline scripts and only the town's own origins plus Paymentus for `form-action`; `X-Frame-Options: DENY`; `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin`; `Permissions-Policy` denying camera, microphone and geolocation.
19. **Security contact and disclosure.** `security@keysvilleva.gov` as a shared mailbox read by two people; a `/.well-known/security.txt` file with the address, the policy page and an expiry date; the disclosure policy already drafted in compliance.md ("Report a security problem"); acknowledgment within 3 business days; the address registered as the security contact in the .gov registrar.
20. **Software hygiene.** WordPress core, theme and plugins kept to a short approved list; auto-updates on for minor and security releases; no abandoned plugins; staging first for major updates; file-integrity monitoring; PHP at a supported version; database credentials unique and rotated when staff leave.

## Part 2(c). Page copy: How we protect your information

**English (under 400 words)**

### How we protect your information

When you use this website, we collect only what we need to answer you or carry out the business you asked about. This page says what that is, who sees it, and how we keep it safe.

**What we ask for.** Most forms ask for your name, a phone number or email, and your request. Permit and license forms ask for the facts on the paper form. We never ask for your full Social Security number, your date of birth, a driver’s license number or a bank or card number online. If a form needs an identity check, we do it in person at Town Hall.

**What becomes public.** Anything you send the town is a public record under the Virginia Freedom of Information Act. Some records are protected: water account details, business tax figures, job applications and requests for a disability accommodation. Before we release any record, a staff member removes personal phone numbers, emails and the names of other people you mentioned.

**Who sees it.** Only the staff who handle your request. A zoning permit goes to the Zoning Administrator. A job application is read by the hiring staff and no one else. Council members can see counts and status, not your details. Every look at a record is logged.

**How we store it.** Your answers travel over an encrypted connection and are stored encrypted. Staff sign in with a password and a code from their phone. We do not send your answers by email; staff get a link instead. Files you upload are scanned before anyone opens them.

**How long we keep it.** Town records are kept for the period set by the Library of Virginia, then destroyed. A zoning permit is kept three years after it expires. A job application is kept three years. A survey answer is kept two years.

**Payments.** Water bills are paid on Paymentus, a separate company. We never see or store card numbers.

**If something goes wrong.** If we ever learn that your information was exposed, we will tell you what happened and what to do, and we will report it to the Commonwealth as the law requires.

## Part 2(d). Town Desk "Security" screen spec

Route `desksecurity`, nav label "Security", icon `shield`, Admin only on the real site (the demo shows it to the signed-in clerk with a note). English only. Sections, top to bottom:

1. **Status strip.** Four chips with words, never color alone: "Two-step sign-in: 4 of 5 accounts", "Backups: last night 2:00 a.m.", "Restore test: passed [date]", "Email protection (DMARC): reject", "HTTPS preload: on". Each chip links down to its section.
2. **Accounts.** Table (caption, scoped headers): name, role, two-step sign-in (On/Off with a switch the Admin can flip; flipping to Off asks for a reason and logs it), last sign-in (date, time, "from Town Hall network" or "from outside"), last access review. Row action "Review" marks the account reviewed today. Rule shown under the table: "Accounts that have not signed in for 90 days are disabled on their own."
3. **Roles matrix.** Table of screens by role: Clerk, Public Works, Treasurer, Council (read-only), Admin. Cells read "Full", "Read", "Own queue" or "No", with an icon. Employment applications: Admin and the hiring role only. Business license receipts: Treasurer and Admin. Settings and Security: Admin.
4. **Sessions.** Idle timeout picker (15, 30, 60 minutes) with the current value, absolute limit shown as text, and "Sign everyone out now" button (logged).
5. **Backups and restore.** Last nightly backup, copies kept (35 daily, 12 monthly), where (off-site, encrypted), last restore test with result and who ran it, "Run restore test" button that in the demo simulates the test, shows the result inline and writes to the activity log. Rule: "A restore test is due every 90 days."
6. **Domain and email.** Chips for HSTS preload, DMARC policy, SPF, DKIM, security.txt, with the record value as text and a "last checked" date.
7. **Access reviews due.** List of accounts not reviewed in 90 days, with a Review button each.
8. **Incident checklist.** The six steps from Part 2(b)(15) as a checklist with the phone numbers, a "Start an incident" button that stamps the time, pins the 24-hour Fusion Center deadline and logs it; "Close incident" asks for the after-action note.
9. **Recent security events.** The last ten activity-log lines that match sign-ins, role changes, settings, restores, releases.

## Part 2(e). Pre-launch security checklist (12 items)

1. HTTPS everywhere, HSTS with preload submitted for keysvilleva.gov, and the old townofkeysvilleva.com redirecting with its own HSTS.
2. SPF `-all`, DKIM on every sender, DMARC `p=reject` with reports going to a mailbox someone reads; the same on any parked town domain.
3. MFA on every WordPress, Town Desk, hosting, registrar (.gov), DNS and email admin account; no shared logins; the vendor's deploy account separate from any staff account.
4. Roles set per the matrix and checked by signing in as each role; Council cannot open a submission; Clerk cannot see gross receipts or employment applications.
5. `ssn4` field removed from the water application (or the authorizing statute cited, Privacy Act § 7 notice added, field encrypted and Treasurer-only); broadband survey says "anonymous", not "confidential".
6. Notifications carry a link and a reference number only; a test submission of every form confirms no answer text reaches any inbox.
7. Upload pipeline tested with a non-PDF renamed `.pdf`, an oversized file, an image with GPS EXIF, and the EICAR test file; all four rejected or cleaned; uploads not reachable by URL.
8. WAF on; `xmlrpc.php`, REST user list, author archives and `?author=` off; login and form rate limits tested; honeypot and timing checks drop a scripted post.
9. Backups: nightly, off-site, encrypted; a restore test completed onto staging and recorded on the Security screen with the date.
10. Audit log writes a line for sign-in, failed sign-in, view, export, redaction, release and delete; exported to CSV once as a test.
11. `security@keysvilleva.gov` live and read by two people; `/.well-known/security.txt` published; the "Report a security problem" page linked in the footer; the address registered at get.gov; the town enrolled in CISA Cyber Hygiene scanning.
12. One-page incident plan signed by the Town Manager with the reportcyber.virginia.gov 24-hour step, the § 18.2-186.6 decision step, vendor and attorney numbers; a copy on paper in Town Hall; the Fusion Center and AG notice steps rehearsed once as a tabletop with staff.
