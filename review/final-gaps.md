## B. Should add

1. **Global print stylesheet.** Only the fridge card, the new-resident checklist and the council one-pager have print rules. A base `@media print` in `10-base.css` that hides the ribbon, header, footer navigation, edit bar, FAB and accessibility panel and prints the main column would make every page printable (minutes pages especially). The pattern is in `33-extras.css`.
2. **Conflict-of-interest and ethics line.** Under "Town Council" on `government`: "Council members file an annual Statement of Economic Interests under Va. Code § 2.2-3115; the forms are public and available from the Town Clerk." Councils are often asked this and the demo is silent.
3. **Budget and audit page.** `taxes` carries a short "Budget and audit" note. A real page listing the adopted budget by year, the annual audit (Va. Code § 15.2-2511 requires an annual audit by an independent CPA; the Auditor of Public Accounts posts locality comparative reports), and the tax rate history would close the transparency gap the current site has.
4. **Ordinance currency statement.** On `documents`, above the Town Code chapters: "The Town Code shown here is the version posted by the town; amendments adopted after [date] are in the minutes until the code is updated. [Town Hall to supply the date of the last codification.]"
5. **Meals tax on the business page.** `business` and the business-license form should say prepared-food sellers collect the 6% meals tax and remit by the 20th (now on `taxes`); add a "Taxes and fees" link in the business page's owner section and in the Business Desk.
6. **Election information block** on `government`: besides the registrar link, give the polling place for the Keysville precinct (`newhere` says the fire department, 500 Osborne Street; confirm), the registration deadline rule (22 days before an election in Virginia, not 29; `newhere` says 29, check it against the registrar), early voting at the registrar's office, and a link to vote.elections.virginia.gov.
7. **Virginia FOIA Council comment form.** `legal-foia` and `records` link the Council's home page; link the actual public-comment page once confirmed, since § 2.2-3704.1(B) asks for the form itself.
8. **.gov identity cue** for the real build: a thin "An official website of the Town of Keysville, Virginia. Here is how you know" strip above the header, like state sites use; the demo ribbon currently occupies that spot.
9. **Town Hall vs Town Office.** The demo says "Town Hall" throughout; the town's own documents say "Town Office" and "the town office located at 120 J Street" (32 times on the Documents page alone). Pick the town's term before launch; it is one string in most files.
10. **Favicon and web manifest** note for the real build (the demo head is a fragment the host wraps). Add the town seal as favicon and a 192/512 px icon with a `manifest.webmanifest` so the site can be pinned to a phone home screen.
11. **Animal control.** `towncounty` sends everything to the county, but Chapter 3 of the Town Code (Animals) is on the Documents page, so the town has its own animal ordinance (running at large, dog licenses may be town or county). Add the county shelter (434-736-6041, 900 Dogwood Road, from the county directory) and confirm who issues dog licenses.
12. **Noise, nuisance and code enforcement.** Chapters 5 (Health and Sanitation) and 6 (Miscellaneous) exist; a help answer "Who do I call about tall grass, junk cars or noise?" pointing to the zoning administrator and the relevant chapter would cover the most common complaint call.
13. **Street lights, mailboxes, dumpsters, cemetery.** Short help answers: street lights are reported through the town (already), mailboxes are USPS and the homeowner's, roll-off dumpsters need [confirm whether a permit or a right-of-way notice], cemeteries in town are [church or private; confirm whether the town owns one].
14. **Senior services, transportation, veterans.** `newhere` has schools and the library; add Piedmont Senior Resources Area Agency on Aging [confirm it covers Charlotte County], the county's transportation options [confirm whether a demand-response van exists], and the Virginia Department of Veterans Services benefits office that serves the county.
15. **Visitors: EV charging and restrooms.** `visit-getting-here` covers parking and fuel; add "There is no public EV charger in town [confirm]; the nearest are in Farmville and South Boston" and the public restroom line (Town Hall during office hours; the library; the convenience center has none).
16. **Calendar subscription.** "Add to my calendar" copies text; the real build should serve an `.ics` feed per calendar filter so phones subscribe once.
17. **Business grants and programs.** On `business` for owners: Virginia Main Street (DHCD) affiliate status, the Virginia Tobacco Region Revitalization Commission (Charlotte County is in its footprint), the SBDC at Longwood, and the county's broadband build-out, each as a link with one sentence.
18. **Vendor registration and bids.** `government-notices` shows a sample bid; add one line on how to be added to the town's vendor list (Virginia Public Procurement Act applies; small purchases are informal) and link eVA for state and local bids.
19. **Sign rules and sidewalk use.** Chapter 7 (Signs, Billboards and Awnings) is on the Documents page; link it from the zoning permit form and the business page ("Putting up a sign needs a zoning permit; the rules are in Chapter 7"). Sidewalk cafe or display permits: confirm whether the town has any rule.
20. **Records retention on tickets.** `legal-records` says each document shows its period; the Town Desk ticket detail could show the GS-19 series and purge date from `security-and-data.md` Part 2(a), so staff see retention where the record lives.
